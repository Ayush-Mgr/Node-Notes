## So what makes a backlink "good"?

I use five questions.

### 1. Is the site relevant?

Agriculture drone company.

Best links:

- Farming websites
- Precision agriculture blogs
- Drone news
- Agricultural universities

Bad links:

- Cooking blog
- Fashion blog
- Random wallpaper site

Relevance matters.

---

### 2. Could a real customer visit?

Ask yourself:

> Would an actual farmer read this?

If yes:

Good.

If nobody would ever visit the page:

Probably weak.

---

### 3. Is there editorial review?

Can literally anyone publish?

Or does someone review submissions?

Editorial review usually increases trust.

---

### 4. Does the page receive traffic?

This is overlooked.

A backlink on a page with:

0 visitors

is less valuable than one that gets:

5,000 visitors/month

because real people can actually discover it.

---

### 5. Does the link make sense?

Example:

> We tested five spraying drones.

Raptor Dynamic performed best.

Learn more here.

That's natural.

Versus:

> Precision agriculture spray drone system crop spraying drones agricultural drone...

Google has become much better at spotting keyword-stuffed, unnatural linking.

# So how do people actually find good sites?

SEO professionals don't just search "guest post".

They use methods like:

### Google search operators

```
"precision agriculture" "write for us"

farming guest post

drone technology contribute

agriculture blog submit article

crop management guest author
```

---

### Competitor backlink analysis

This is the most common approach.

Tools like:

- Ahrefs
- Semrush
- Moz

Let you inspect where competitors are getting links.

If three competitors all have a backlink from the same agriculture publication, that's a strong prospect.

---

### Industry organizations

Examples:

- Farming associations
- Agricultural universities
- Government agriculture resources
- Industry directories
- Equipment suppliers

These are often excellent sources of relevant, trusted links.

---

# Here's something that changed my own thinking about SEO

Think less about:

> "How do I build a backlink?"

Think more about:

> "Why would someone _want_ to link to this page?"

If your content genuinely answers a question, provides original data, or solves a problem, earning links becomes much easier.

That's why content marketing and digital PR have become so important.
