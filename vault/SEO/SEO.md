### The important mental model

Don't think:

> **"One keyword = one page."**

Think:

> **"One search intent = one primary page."**

A keyword can have several related keywords, but if they represent the **same search intent**, they generally belong to the same primary page.

And blogs aren't simply **"more pages = more traffic."**

They're a way to capture **additional search intents around your commercial topic** and then funnel relevant users toward your important pages.

That's the foundation of **topic clusters + internal linking**.

---
## Search intent
**Search intent = what the person actually wants when they search a keyword.**

This is the key concept that solves your blog-vs-target-page confusion.

### 4 main types

|Intent|What the user wants|Example|
|---|---|---|
|**Informational**|Learn something|`what is GoHighLevel CRM`|
|**Commercial**|Research before buying|`GoHighLevel vs HubSpot`|
|**Transactional**|Buy/hire/do something|`GoHighLevel consultant Brisbane`|
|**Navigational**|Find a specific site/brand|`GoHighLevel login`|

### Why this matters for your SEO pages

Suppose your target URL is:

**`/gohighlevel-consultant-brisbane/`**

Keyword: **GoHighLevel consultant Brisbane**

The intent is **transactional** → _"I want to hire someone."_

So your target page should own that intent.

Then create blogs around **different intents**:

- `what is GoHighLevel` → informational
    
- `how does GoHighLevel work` → informational
    
- `GoHighLevel vs HubSpot` → commercial
    
- `GoHighLevel pricing` → commercial
    
- `how to automate lead follow-up with GoHighLevel` → informational
    

And those blogs can internally link to:

**GoHighLevel Consultant Brisbane → "Need help implementing GoHighLevel?"**

So the flow becomes:

**Informational search → Blog → Internal link → Commercial/transactional page → Conversion**

That's much better than:

**Keyword → 5 pages targeting exactly the same thing → Google decides which URL to rank.**

Imagine these searches:

- `best CRM for small business`
- `best CRM software for small businesses`
- `CRM for small companies`
- `top CRM for small business`

Different keywords, but the searcher essentially wants the **same thing**:

> **"Help me choose a CRM for my small business."**

That's **one intent**, not four.
