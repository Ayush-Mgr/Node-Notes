# bias 

Class imbalance is usually a quantity problem that can occur naturally, while bias is a representation or fairness problem that often comes from human decisions, data collection methods, or historical prejudice
 **### Causes of Bias in Big Data / ML

#### 1. Sampling Bias

Data collected does not represent the whole population.

Example:

```text
Survey only college students
→ conclusions about all adults
```

---

#### 2. Historical Bias

Past discrimination already exists in the data.

Example:

```text
Past hiring favored men
```

Model learns the same pattern.

---

#### 3. Representation Bias

Some groups have too few examples.

Example:

```text
95% adults
5% children
```

Model performs poorly on children.

---

#### 4. Measurement Bias

Data is collected incorrectly.

Example:

```text
Faulty sensors
Incorrect labels
Wrong survey responses
```

---

#### 5. Algorithmic Bias

The algorithm itself produces unfair outcomes.

Example:

```text
Model gives lower loan approvals
to certain groups
```

---

### Easy Memory

```text
Bias can come from:

Data Collection
↓
Data Representation
↓
Historical Data
↓
Measurement Errors
↓
Algorithms
```

### 2-Mark Exam Answer

> The main causes of bias in Big Data analysis are sampling bias, historical bias, representation bias, measurement bias, and algorithmic bias. These biases can lead to unfair, inaccurate, or discriminatory outcomes.**

## Fairness

**Definition:**

> Fairness means ensuring that a model or data-driven system does not unjustly favor or disadvantage any individual or group.

---

### Simple Example

Suppose a loan approval model:

```text
Men approval rate    = 90%
Women approval rate  = 40%
```

If both groups have similar qualifications, the model is unfair.

---

### Relationship with Bias

```text
Bias  → Cause
Fairness → Goal
```

We reduce bias to achieve fairness.

---

### Real-World Examples

- Hiring systems
    
- Loan approvals
    
- Healthcare diagnosis
    
- College admissions
    

A fair model should judge people based on relevant factors, not gender, race, age, etc.

---

### Exam Answer

> Fairness in Big Data analysis refers to ensuring that data, algorithms, and decisions do not unfairly discriminate against individuals or groups and that outcomes are equitable for everyone.

### One-Line Memory Trick

```text
Bias = Unfairness

```

or

> **Fairness means giving similar people similar outcomes regardless of group membership.**
