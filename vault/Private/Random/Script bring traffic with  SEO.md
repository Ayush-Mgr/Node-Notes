## 1. AUDIT — Check the foundations

Before trying to get traffic, we need to check whether there are any basic ==problems== stopping people from finding our website.

[Visual: A clipboard appears with checkboxes. Each item gets checked as we cover it.]

- Can Google discover and index my important pages?
	- site:yourdomain.com
	- yourdomain.com/sitemaps.xml
	- yourdomain.com/robots.txt
    
- Does each important page target something people actually search for?


We'll go through these checks one by one.
### First: Can Google find your website?

[Visual: Open Google and search `site:yourdomain.com`.]

Search for `site:yourdomain.com`, replacing the domain with your own website.

If pages appear, Google has indexed at least some of your website.

If nothing appears, don't panic. First, double-check that you entered the correct domain and that your website is live.

If everything looks fine, open Google Search Console and add and verify your website.

Use URL Inspection to check your important pages and find out whether Google has indexed them. If a page isn't indexed, investigate why, fix any problems, and request indexing when appropriate.

[Visual: Show Google Search Console.]

Next, check whether your website has an XML sitemap. 

A sitemap is basically a map that gives Google a list of the important pages on your website, so it can discover them more easily.

Think of it like giving Google a list of addresses to explore instead of making it find every page on its own.

So, check whether your website has a sitemap, usually at `yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml`, and submit it through Google Search Console.

You should also check your `robots.txt` file to make sure you haven't accidentally blocked important pages from being crawled.

which could be done by searching searching `yourdomain.com/robots.txt`


Finally, check Search Console's indexing report for issues such as accidental `noindex` directives. Remember, only remove these from pages you actually want Google to index.

That's the first step: make sure your website is accessible to Google.

### Second: Does every important page target something people actually search for?

Don't build a page and then randomly stuff keywords into it.

First, decide what someone would search for to find that page.

In SEO, we call this **search intent**.

There are four common types.

[Visual: Display a simple table with examples.]

- **Informational:** The person wants to learn something. Example: "Is GTA 6 even coming?"
    
- **Commercial:** The person is researching options before deciding. Example: "GTA 6 vs GTA 5"
    
- **Transactional:** The person wants to take an action. Example: "Pre-order GTA 6 "
    
- **Navigational:** The person wants to find a particular website or brand. Example: "GTA 6 official Rockstar website"
    

Now, imagine you're building a business website.

Your `/web-design` page should explain your web design services.

Your `/seo` page should explain your SEO services.

And your `/about` page should tell visitors about your business.

Each important page needs a clear purpose and should satisfy the searcher's intent.

We'll use this idea again when we research keywords.

## 2. KEYWORD RESEARCH — Find what people search for

So, what exactly are keywords?

They're the words or phrases people type into search engines when looking for information, products, or services.

Some are broad, like "SEO."

Others are more specific, like "best SEO agency for a small business."

[Visual: Animate the broad keyword expanding into a more specific phrase.]

These more specific searches are often called long-tail keywords.

But how do you know which keywords to target?

That's where keyword research tools come in.

You can start with free features from tools like Semrush's Keyword Magic Tool or Ahrefs' Keyword Generator.

I like starting with a core keyword related to the website. For example, if you're offering web design services, search for "web design."

The tool will suggest related keywords and provide useful metrics.

Here are the main things to look at.

**First, relevance.** Does this keyword actually relate to your website and what you offer?

**Second, search intent.** Does the person searching want the kind of information or service your page provides?

**Third, search volume.** This estimates how many searches a keyword receives per month.

**Fourth, keyword difficulty, or KD.** This is a tool's estimate of how difficult it might be to rank for that keyword.

If you're working with a new website, targeting extremely competitive keywords right away may be difficult.

Instead, look for relevant, more specific keywords with realistic competition and enough search demand to make them worthwhile.

But don't blindly trust the numbers.

Search the keyword on Google yourself and examine the results. Are established brands dominating the results? Are the ranking pages actually similar to yours? Can you create something that genuinely answers the search better for your intended audience?

The goal isn't simply to find a keyword with high volume and low difficulty.

**It's to find relevant search opportunities that your website has a realistic chance of competing for.**

Once you've selected a target, map it to the appropriate page instead of creating multiple pages for tiny variations of the same search.

## 3. ON-PAGE SEO — Optimize your pages

Now that we know what a page should target, let's check whether the page communicates that topic clearly.

Start with these basics.

**Title:** Does the title describe the page, match the search intent, and naturally include the main keyword?

**H1 heading:** Is there a clear main heading that tells visitors what the page is about?

**Meta description:** Does it accurately summarize the page and give searchers a useful reason to click?

**URL:** Is the URL descriptive and understandable?

**Content:** Does the page actually answer the searcher's question or provide the service they're looking for?

**Images:** Use descriptive alt text for meaningful images. Describe what's actually in the image rather than stuffing it with keywords.

[Visual: Show an AI-generated page, then highlight the title, H1, description, URL and content.]

The important thing is to use your primary keyword naturally where it makes sense. Don't repeat it everywhere just to make a page look optimized.

Your content should be useful to the person reading it, not written exclusively for search engines.

One more thing: connect related pages with useful internal links.

For example, an article about choosing a web design service could link to your web design service page. That service page could then link to your contact page.

This helps visitors navigate your website and helps search engines discover related pages.

## 4. OFF-PAGE SEO — Get your website discovered

So far, we've worked on the website itself. Now we need to get it in front of people.

Start with the social media platforms where your potential customers actually spend their time.

Share useful posts related to your products or services, and when relevant, link to the appropriate page on your website.

For example:

**Instagram:** Share a project or demonstrate your service, with a link to the relevant page.

**LinkedIn:** Share a useful article, case study, or professional insight.

**Facebook groups and Reddit:** Participate in relevant discussions and share your website only when it genuinely helps answer someone's question and follows the community rules.

Don't paste your URL everywhere just to create links. Instead, find topics and discussions your potential visitors are already interested in.

For example, imagine you run a gaming website. You could create a useful article like "Which GTA Games Are Worth Playing Before GTA 6?" and share it in relevant gaming communities where self-promotion is allowed.

Or, if someone is asking which GTA game they should play, answer their question and link to your guide if it genuinely helps.

The idea is simple: **find the right audience, provide something useful, and give interested people a reason to click through to your website.** That can bring relevant referral traffic while helping more people discover your content.

## 5. MEASURE — Check whether your work is helping

[Visual: Return to Google Search Console.]

Finally, don't just make changes and forget about them.

Return to Google Search Console and monitor three important metrics.

**Impressions:** How often are your pages appearing in Google Search?

**Clicks:** How many clicks are those pages receiving from search results?

**Queries:** What are people actually searching for when your website appears?

You can use these insights to identify pages that are gaining visibility, queries you didn't expect, and pages that might need improvement.

If you also have analytics set up, check whether visitors are taking meaningful actions, such as submitting a form, requesting a quote, or making a purchase.

SEO takes time, and results aren't guaranteed. Keep publishing useful content where there's a genuine need, maintain your website, and use the data to decide what to improve next.

You need to make sure Google can discover it, understand what people are searching for, optimize your pages around those needs, and promote your website where your potential audience actually is.
