Evaluating your model's prediction errors is a guaranteed high-value target on your exam. In your study guide, this covers **5-Mark Question #9** (_"Explain MAE, MSE, RMSE, and MAPE"_) and **15-Mark Question #3** (_"Discuss various forecasting accuracy measures and their interpretation"_) in Module 5.

When writing your exam answer, explain that all accuracy measures start with the same foundational building block: **The Forecast Error ($e_t$)**.

$$e_t = \text{Actual Value } (Y_t) - \text{Forecast Value } (F_t)$$

Because raw errors can be positive or negative, adding them up directly would cause them to cancel each other out to zero. The four accuracy metrics use different mathematical tricks to solve this canceling problem.

### The 4 Core Accuracy Measures

#### 1. MAE (Mean Absolute Error)

- **What it does:** It takes the absolute value (strips away the negative signs) of all errors and finds their arithmetic average.
    
- **Formula:**
    
    $$\text{MAE} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{t=1}^{n} |Y_t - F_t|$$
    
- **Exam Interpretation:** MAE measures the average magnitude of your errors in the **exact same units** as your original data (e.g., "Our sales forecast is off by an average of 12 shirts"). It treats all errors equally—an error of 10 is punished exactly twice as much as an error of 5.
    

#### 2. MSE (Mean Squared Error)

- **What it does:** It squares every error before averaging them.
    
- **Formula:**
    
    $$\text{MSE} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{t=1}^{n} (Y_t - F_t)^2$$
    
- **Exam Interpretation:** Because squaring numbers makes large numbers explode exponentially ($2^2 = 4$, but $10^2 = 100$), **MSE heavily penalizes massive outliers**. If your model makes a huge mistake on even a single day, the MSE will skyrocket.
    
- **Major Flaw:** Its units are squared (e.g., "Our error is 144 _squared shirts_"), which is impossible to explain to a business manager.
    

#### 3. [RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sA1K22Hmh1g)

- **What it does:** It simply takes the square root of the MSE to fix the weird squared units.
    
- **Formula:**
    
    $$\text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n} \sum_{t=1}^{n} (Y_t - F_t)^2}$$
    
- **Exam Interpretation:** RMSE is the industry standard for data science competitions. Like MAE, it is expressed in original data units, but like MSE, it **retains extreme sensitivity to large outliers**.
    

#### 4. MAPE (Mean Absolute Percentage Error)

- **What it does:** It converts every error into a percentage of the actual observation before averaging.
    
- **Formula:**
    
    $$\text{MAPE} = \frac{100\%}{n} \sum_{t=1}^{n} \left| \frac{Y_t - F_t}{Y_t} \right|$$
    
- **Exam Interpretation:** MAPE is **scale-independent**. It lets you compare the accuracy of models across completely different datasets (e.g., comparing error rates between a luxury car dealership and a grocery store). Business executives love MAPE because saying _"Our forecast has a 4.2% error rate"_ is universally understood.
    
- **Major Flaw:** If any actual value in your dataset is zero ($Y_t = 0$), the formula requires dividing by zero and **completely breaks**.
    

### The 15-Mark "Pro Tip" for Your Exam

If you get the 15-mark interpretation question, impress your professor by writing down this golden rule of error comparison:

> **$\text{RMSE}$ will always be greater than or equal to $\text{MAE}$ ($\text{RMSE} \ge \text{MAE}$).**

- If $\text{RMSE}$ is **almost identical** to $\text{MAE}$, your model makes consistent, uniform errors every day.
    
- If $\text{RMSE}$ is **significantly larger** than $\text{MAE}$, your model suffers from extreme variance volatility—meaning it usually predicts well, but occasionally drops a massive, catastrophic blunder!
