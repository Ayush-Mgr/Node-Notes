### 1. ARCH Model (Autoregressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity)

- **Definition:** A model used to forecast **Volatility (Variance)**. Standard models assume variance stays constant over time (Homoscedasticity). ARCH assumes variance changes over time and that today's volatility is driven by the **squares of previous past errors (shocks)**.
    
- **Formula for ARCH(q):**
    
    $$\sigma_t^2 = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1 \epsilon_{t-1}^2 + \alpha_2 \epsilon_{t-2}^2 + \dots + \alpha_q \epsilon_{t-q}^2$$
    
    _(Where_ $\sigma_t^2$ _is today's predicted variance, and_ $\epsilon_{t-i}^2$ _are the past squared errors)._
    
- **Application:** Used heavily in finance to model "Volatility Clustering"—periods where stock markets swing wildly for a few days, followed by periods of calm.
    

### 2. GARCH Model (Generalized ARCH)

- **Definition:** An upgraded version of ARCH. Instead of just looking at past _errors_, GARCH assumes that today's volatility is driven by **past squared errors AND past variances**.
    
- **Formula for GARCH(p,q):**
    
    $$\sigma_t^2 = \alpha_0 + \underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^q \alpha_i \epsilon_{t-i}^2}_{\text{ARCH part (Past Errors)}} + \underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^p \beta_j \sigma_{t-j}^2}_{\text{GARCH part (Past Variances)}}$$
- **Properties of GARCH(p,q):** * $p$: The number of past variances used.
    
    - $q$: The number of past squared errors used.
        
- **Application:** It is the industry standard for risk management and options pricing because it captures long-term volatility memory.
    

### 15-Mark Comparison: ARCH vs. GARCH

If asked to compare these two on your exam, focus on **Efficiency (Parsimony)**:

- **ARCH** only looks at past shocks. To capture a long period of volatility, an ARCH model might need to look back 10 or 15 days (e.g., ARCH(15)), making the math bloated and prone to overfitting.
    
- **GARCH** looks at past shocks _and_ past variances. Because past variances already contain the "summary" of older shocks, a simple **GARCH(1,1)** model can achieve the exact same accuracy as a massive ARCH(15) model, but with far fewer variables to calculate.
    

### 3. SARIMA (Seasonal ARIMA)

- **Definition:** An extension of the ARIMA model that explicitly handles **Seasonality** (regular, repeating patterns like high retail sales every December).
    
- **Structure:** It combines a standard ARIMA model with a seasonal ARIMA model. Written as: $\text{ARIMA}(p,d,q) \times (P,D,Q)_s$.
    
    - Lowercase letters are for the non-seasonal trend.
        
    - Uppercase letters are for the seasonal pattern.
        
    - $s$ is the season length (e.g., $s=12$ for monthly data).
        
- **Formula Representation:** Using the Backshift operator ($B$), the mathematical structure multiplies the standard ARIMA polynomials by seasonal polynomials:
    
    $$\underbrace{\Phi_P(B^s) \phi_p(B)}_{\text{AR terms}} \underbrace{(1-B^s)^D (1-B)^d}_{\text{Differencing}} Y_t = \underbrace{\Theta_Q(B^s) \theta_q(B)}_{\text{MA terms}} \epsilon_t$$

### 15-Mark Comparison: ARIMA vs. SARIMA

- **Standard ARIMA** cannot process data that has repeating cycles. If you feed it seasonal data, the ACF chart will show a "wavy" pattern that never dies, and the model will fail its diagnostic checks.
    
- **SARIMA** solves this by adding **Seasonal Differencing** ($D$). Instead of subtracting yesterday from today, it subtracts _last year's December_ from _this year's December_ to flatten the repeating wave, allowing accurate forecasting of seasonal products.
    

### 4. VAR (Vector Autoregression)

- **Definition:** A multivariate forecasting model used when two or more time series influence **each other simultaneously**.
    
- **How it works:** In standard regression, $X$ predicts $Y$. But what if $Y$ also predicts $X$? (e.g., Personal Income drives Consumer Spending, but Consumer Spending also drives Personal Income).
    
- **The "Vector" element:** VAR creates a system of equations where every variable is predicted using its own past lags AND the past lags of every other variable in the system. It captures **Bidirectional Relationships**.
    
- **Formula for a 2-Variable VAR(1):**
    
    $$Y_{1,t} = c_1 + \phi_{11} Y_{1,t-1} + \phi_{12} Y_{2,t-1} + \epsilon_{1,t}$$$$Y_{2,t} = c_2 + \phi_{21} Y_{1,t-1} + \phi_{22} Y_{2,t-1} + \epsilon_{2,t}$$
    
    _(Notice how to predict_ $Y_1$ _today, we use yesterday's_ $Y_1$ _AND yesterday's_ $Y_2$_)_
