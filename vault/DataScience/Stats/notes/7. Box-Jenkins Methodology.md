The Box-Jenkins methodology is a systematic, iterative flowchart used to identify, estimate, and evaluate an ARIMA($p, d, q$) model for forecasting time series data.


### Step 1: Identification (Finding $p, d, q$)

This is the setup phase where you prepare the data and choose the model's structure using visual charts and statistical tests.

- **Determine 'd' (Differencing):** The data _must_ be stationary. You use the Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) test or look at a time plot to check for trends. If a trend exists, you calculate differences (e.g., Today - Yesterday) until the data is flat. The number of times you do this is your $d$.
    
- **Determine 'p' and 'q' (Model Order):** Once stationary, you examine the ACF and PACF correlograms:
    
    - **AR(**$p$**):** PACF drops off a cliff after lag $p$, while ACF fades slowly.
        
    - **MA(**$q$**):** ACF drops off a cliff after lag $q$, while PACF fades slowly.
        
    - **ARMA(**$p, q$**):** Both fade slowly.
        

### Step 2: Estimation (Crunching the Math)

Once you have identified the structure (for example, ARIMA(2, 1, 1)), you hand it over to the computer.

- In this step, mathematical algorithms like **Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)** or **Ordinary Least Squares (OLS)** are used to calculate the exact numerical weights for the AR coefficients ($\phi$) and the MA coefficients ($\theta$).
    
- The goal is to find the exact weights that minimize the prediction errors.
    

### Step 3: Diagnostic Checking (The Sanity Check)

Before you trust the model, you must prove that it actually worked. You do this by performing **Residual Analysis**.

- You extract the errors (residuals) from your model—the leftover variance that the model failed to predict.
    
- **The White Noise Test:** You plot the ACF chart of these _leftover errors_. If your model is good, the ACF chart should look completely blank (all lags trapped inside the blue noise threshold).
    
- **Ljung-Box Test:** A statistical test to confirm the residuals are just random white noise.
    
- _If the residuals are not white noise, your model failed. You must loop back to Step 1 and try different_ $p$ _and_ $q$_values._
    

### Step 4: Forecasting

Once the model passes the diagnostic check in Step 3, the iterative loop is closed. The validated ARIMA model is finally used to project future values and create confidence intervals for those predictions.
