### Part 1: Core Forecasting Methods (5-Mark Questions)

#### 1. Simple Moving Average (SMA)

- **Definition:** A forecasting technique that predicts the next value by calculating the unweighted arithmetic mean of the last $k$ actual data points.
    
- **Formula:**
    
    $$F_{t+1} = \frac{A_t + A_{t-1} + \dots + A_{t-k+1}}{k}$$
    
- **Key Characteristic:** It treats all $k$ past observations as equally important. Older data outside the window $k$ is dropped completely (has zero weight).
    

#### 2. Weighted Moving Average (WMA)

- **Definition:** A variation of the moving average where recent data points are assigned higher mathematical weights than older data points.
    
- **Formula:**
    
    $$F_{t+1} = w_1 A_t + w_2 A_{t-1} + \dots + w_k A_{t-k+1}$$
    
    _(Note: The sum of all weights must equal 1: $\sum w_i = 1$)_
    
- **Key Characteristic:** It responds much faster to recent demand changes than a simple moving average.
    

#### 3. [Single Exponential Smoothing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k_HN0wOKDd0) (SES)

- **Definition:** A forecasting method that weights past observations with exponentially decreasing weights to forecast future values.
    
- **Formula:**
    
    $$F_{t+1} = \alpha A_t + (1 - \alpha) F_t$$
    
    Where $\alpha$ (Alpha) is the **smoothing constant** ($0 < \alpha < 1$).
    
- **Key Characteristic:** Unlike moving averages that drop old data, SES retains all past historical data in its "memory," but older events have diminishing influence over time.
- for the first forcast $F_1$ vaule : $F_1 = A_1$ 
    

#### 4. Holt’s Exponential Smoothing (Double Exponential Smoothing)

- **Definition:** An extension of single exponential smoothing that adds a second equation to capture **Linear Trend** in the data.
    
- **Equations:**
    
    1. **Level:** $L_t = \alpha A_t + (1 - \alpha)(L_{t-1} + T_{t-1})$
        
    2. **Trend:** $T_t = \beta (L_t - L_{t-1}) + (1 - \beta) T_{t-1}$
        
    3. **Forecast ($m$ steps ahead):** $F_{t+m} = L_t + m T_t$
        
- **Key Characteristic:** Uses two smoothing constants: $\alpha$ for the baseline level and $\beta$ (Beta) for the trend slope.
    

#### 5. Holt-Winters’ Forecasting (Triple Exponential Smoothing)

- **Definition:** An advanced smoothing technique that captures **Base Level**, **Linear Trend**, and **Seasonality**simultaneously.
    
- **Key Characteristic:** Introduces a third equation and a third smoothing constant, $\gamma$ (Gamma), to scale seasonal repeating patterns (e.g., spike in sales every December). It can be applied as an **Additive** model (constant seasonal volume) or a **Multiplicative** model (proportional seasonal growth).

_If you get the 15-mark question asking you to compare these methods, draw this exact table on your exam paper:_

|**Feature**|**Simple Moving Average (SMA)**|**Weighted Moving Average (WMA)**|**Single Exponential Smoothing (SES)**|**Holt’s Model**|**Holt-Winters’ Model**|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|**Data Memory**|Only last $k$ periods|Only last $k$periods|Infinite (all past data)|Infinite|Infinite|
|**Weights**|Equal ($1/k$) for all periods|User-defined (Higher for recent)|Exponentially decaying ($\alpha$)|Exponential ($\alpha, \beta$)|Exponential ($\alpha, \beta, \gamma$)|
|**Handles Trend?**|No (Lags behind trend)|Poorly (Still lags slightly)|No (Lags behind trend)|**Yes**|**Yes**|
|**Handles Seasonality?**|No|No|No|No|**Yes**|
|**Parameters Needed**|$k$(Window size)|$k$ and Weights ($w_i$)|$\alpha$|$\alpha, \beta$|$\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ and Season Length ($L$)|
|**Best Used For**|Stationary, flat demand|Flat demand with short-term shifts|Stationary data with random noise|Data with steady upward/downward trend|Complex business data with trend & seasonal peaks|

#### Numerical #4 (Page 5): Exponential Smoothing Calculation

**Question:** Given actual values $Y_1 = 50, Y_2 = 60, Y_3 = 55$, smoothing constant $\alpha = 0.3$, and Initial Forecast $F_1 = 50$.

1. _Compute forecasts for periods 2, 3, and 4._
    
2. _Show calculation steps._
    
3. _Comment on the smoothing effect._
    
4. _State when exponential smoothing is preferred._
    

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

Formula: $F_{t+1} = \alpha Y_t + (1 - \alpha) F_t$

Given: $\alpha = 0.3$, therefore $(1 - \alpha) = 0.7$

- **Period 2 Forecast ($F_2$):**
    
    $$F_2 = 0.3(Y_1) + 0.7(F_1)$$
    
    $$F_2 = 0.3(50) + 0.7(50) = 15 + 35 = \mathbf{50}$$
    
- **Period 3 Forecast ($F_3$):**
    
    $$F_3 = 0.3(Y_2) + 0.7(F_2)$$
    
    $$F_3 = 0.3(60) + 0.7(50) = 18 + 35 = \mathbf{53}$$
    
- **Period 4 Forecast ($F_4$):**
    
    $$F_4 = 0.3(Y_3) + 0.7(F_3)$$
    
    $$F_4 = 0.3(55) + 0.7(53) = 16.5 + 37.1 = \mathbf{53.6}$$
    

**Answers to Theory Parts:**

- **Comment on smoothing effect:** The actual data jumped from 50 to 60 (+10), but our forecast only rose to 53 (+3). Because $\alpha = 0.3$ is relatively small, the model heavily anchors to historical averages (70% weight) and dampens extreme volatility or random spikes.
    
- **When preferred:** Exponential smoothing is preferred when data is relatively stationary (flat), storage capacity is limited (you don't need to store long arrays of historical data), and fast automated forecasting is required for inventory or sales management.
