# 1. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

**Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)** is the process of examining and visualizing data to discover patterns, trends, relationships, and anomalies before applying statistical or machine learning models.

EDA helps understand the structure and quality of data.

## Objectives of EDA

- Understand the dataset.
    
- Detect missing values.
    
- Identify outliers.
    
- Find relationships between variables.
    
- Check data distribution.
    
- Generate hypotheses for further analysis.
    

---

## Steps in EDA

1. Data Collection
    
2. Data Cleaning
    
3. Summary Statistics (mean, median, mode, etc.)
    
4. Data Visualization
    
5. Pattern and Trend Analysis
    
6. Outlier Detection
    
7. Feature Selection
    

---

## Common EDA Techniques

### Univariate Analysis

Analyzes one variable at a time.

**Charts:**

- Histogram
    
- Box Plot
    
- Bar Chart
    

### Bivariate Analysis

Analyzes the relationship between two variables.

**Charts:**

- Scatter Plot
    
- Line Chart
    
- Grouped Bar Chart
    

### Multivariate Analysis

Analyzes three or more variables.

**Charts:**

- Heat Map
    
- Bubble Chart
    
- Parallel Coordinates Plot
    

---

## Importance of EDA

- Improves data quality
    
- Detects errors early
    
- Supports better model building
    
- Helps identify useful features
    
- Reveals hidden insights

# 2. Visual Analytics

**Visual Analytics** combines **data visualization, analytics, and human interaction** to help users analyze large and complex datasets.

Unlike static charts, visual analytics allows users to interact with data using filters, zoom, drill-down, and dashboards.

---

## Components of Visual Analytics

### Data

Raw information from various sources.

### Visualization

Charts, graphs, maps, and dashboards.

### Analytics

Statistical analysis and machine learning.

### Interaction

Filtering, zooming, drill-down, and tooltips.

---

## Features

- Interactive dashboards
    
- Real-time data analysis
    
- Predictive analytics
    
- Data filtering
    
- Dynamic visualizations
    
- Decision support
    

---

## Applications

- Business analytics
    
- Healthcare
    
- Finance
    
- Cybersecurity
    
- Marketing
    
- Government analytics

# 3. Business Intelligence (BI)

**Business Intelligence (BI)** is the process of collecting, analyzing, and visualizing business data to support strategic and operational decision-making.

BI transforms raw business data into useful information.

---

## BI Process

```text
Data Collection
       ↓
Data Warehouse
       ↓
Data Analysis
       ↓
Dashboard & Reports
       ↓
Business Decisions
```

---

## Components of BI

### Data Sources

- Databases
    
- Excel files
    
- ERP systems
    
- CRM systems
    
- Cloud services
    

### Data Warehouse

Central repository that stores integrated business data.

### Analytics

Processes data to identify trends and insights.

### Reporting

Creates reports and dashboards.

### Decision Making

Uses insights to improve business performance.

---

## BI Tools

- Microsoft Power BI
    
- Tableau
    
- Qlik Sense
    
- SAP BusinessObjects
    
- Google Looker Studio
    

---

## Applications

- Sales analysis
    
- Customer analytics
    
- Inventory management
    
- Financial reporting
    
- Marketing performance
    
- Human Resource analytics
    

---

## Benefits

- Better decision-making
    
- Increased efficiency
    
- Real-time reporting
    
- Improved productivity
    
- Competitive advantage

# 4. Scientific Visualization

**Scientific Visualization** is the graphical representation of scientific and engineering data to better understand physical phenomena and complex datasets.

It often deals with large numerical datasets generated through experiments or simulations.

---

## Characteristics

- Large datasets
    
- 2D and 3D visualizations
    
- Simulation-based
    
- High precision
    
- Interactive exploration
    

---

## Common Scientific Visualizations

### Line Graph

Shows trends over time.

### Contour Plot

Represents constant values using contour lines.

### Surface Plot

Displays three-dimensional surfaces.

### Volume Rendering

Visualizes internal structures of 3D objects.

### Vector Field

Shows direction and magnitude of forces.

### Streamline Plot

Visualizes fluid or airflow.

---

## Applications

- Weather forecasting
    
- Medical imaging (CT, MRI)
    
- Engineering simulations
    
- Astronomy
    
- Climate research
    
- Physics and chemistry
    
- Fluid dynamics
    

---

## Scientific Visualization Tools

- MATLAB
    
- ParaView
    
- VTK (Visualization Toolkit)
    
- Python (Matplotlib, Plotly, Mayavi)
    
- Tecplot

# Exam Answer (5 Marks)

### Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

EDA is the process of exploring and summarizing data using statistics and visualizations to identify patterns, trends, missing values, and outliers before further analysis.

### Visual Analytics

Visual analytics combines **data visualization, analytics, and interactive tools** to analyze complex datasets. It includes dashboards, filters, drill-down, and real-time insights.

### Business Intelligence (BI)

Business Intelligence (BI) collects, analyzes, and visualizes business data to support informed decision-making. Common BI tools include **Power BI, Tableau, and Looker Studio**.

### Scientific Visualization

Scientific visualization represents scientific and engineering data using 2D and 3D graphics such as **surface plots, contour plots, and volume rendering**. It is widely used in medicine, engineering, climate science, and physics.
