[[DataScience/Stats/notes/Autocorrelation |Autocorrelation]] Function (ACF) & Partial Autocorrelation Function (PCF) how much of the past we need to predict the future. If you want to predict tomorrow's stock price or web traffic, do you need to look at just yesterday, or do you need to look at the last 5 days?

## [[datascience/stats/notes/1. intro - time series analysis|Stationary]]

there are two types: Strong and Weak. 99% of real world problems are weak types.

## ACF
here the middle lag matters 
```mermaid
graph LR
    X3[Xₜ₋₃] --> X2[Xₜ₋₂]
    X2 --> X1[Xₜ₋₁]
    X1 --> XT[Xₜ]

 

    ACF[ACF keeps direct + indirect paths]
```
formula:
 
$$
\rho_k =
\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X_t,X_{t-k})}
{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X_t)\operatorname{Var}(X_{t-k})}}
$$
> Same as [[DataScience/Stats/notes/6. Correlation coefficient (r)|correlation]] formula as acf means the auto-correlation of its past lags 


or for staionary data:

$$
\rho_k = \frac{\gamma_k}{\gamma_0}
$$

where:  $\gamma_k = \operatorname{Cov}(X_t,X_{t-k})$

## PACF

here the middle lags doesn't matter

```mermaid
graph LR
    X3[Xₜ₋₃] -.-> X2[Xₜ₋₂]
    X2 -.-> X1[Xₜ₋₁]
    X1 -.-> XT[Xₜ]

    X3 -- Direct relationship --> XT

    PACF[PACF removes indirect paths and keeps only direct path]
```
formula:

$$
\alpha_k =
\operatorname{Corr}
\left(
X_t,
X_{t-k}
\mid
X_{t-1},X_{t-2},\ldots,X_{t-k+1}
\right)
$$
- The PACF at lag k is the correlation between $X_t$  and $X_{t−k}$	after removing the effects of intermediate lags

example for lag3:

$$
\alpha_3 =
\operatorname{Corr}
\left(
X_t,
X_{t-3}
\mid
X_{t-1},X_{t-2}
\right)
$$

## Model selection with chart 

### The Golden Rule: _"The Cliff holds the integer"_

When looking at time series charts, whichever chart **drops off a cliff** into the blue noise zone is the one that hands you your order number ($p$ or $q$). Whichever chart **fades away slowly** (like a wavy slide or a staircase) is just telling you which family the model belongs to.

### 1. The AR($p$) Signature (Autoregressive)

- **The Logic:** Past _data_ drives the future.
    
- **ACF Chart:** Fades away slowly (exponential decay or wavy sine wave).
    
- **PACF Chart:** **Drops dead** instantly after Lag $p$.
    

![[AR_chart.png]]

- **Exam Answer:** _"For an AR(p) process, the ACF tails off gradually while the PACF cuts off abruptly after lag p."_
    

### 2. The MA($q$) Signature (Moving Average)

- **The Logic:** Past _random shocks_ drive the future.
    
- **ACF Chart:** **Drops dead** instantly after Lag $q$.
    
- **PACF Chart:** Fades away slowly (exponential decay or wavy sine wave).
    

![[MA_chart.png]]

- **Exam Answer:** _"For an MA(q) process, the PACF tails off gradually while the ACF cuts off abruptly after lag q."_
    

### 3. The ARMA($p, q$) Signature (Combined)

- **The Logic:** Both momentum AND shocks are happening at the exact same time.
    
- **ACF Chart:** Fades away slowly.
    
- **PACF Chart:** Fades away slowly.
    

![[ARMA_chart.png]]

- **Exam Answer:** _"For an ARMA process, neither chart cuts off; both the ACF and PACF tail off gradually."_Because there is no visual cliff, human eyes cannot find $p$ or $q$. Write on your paper: _"We must use automated Grid Search and Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) to find the mathematically optimal $p$ and $q$."_
    

### Summary Cheat Sheet to sketch on your paper:

|**Process**|**ACF Chart**|**PACF Chart**|
|---|---|---|
|**AR($p$)**|Tails off|**Cuts off at $p$**|
|**MA($q$)**|**Cuts off at $q$**|Tails off|
|**ARMA($p,q$)**|Tails off|Tails off|

If you draw those exact 6 box-sketches next to your written explanation on exam day, any statistics professor will instantly award you the full 15 marks.
